## Why I Built This Analytical Framework

Most TikTok agency reporting failures are not data failures. They are analytical failures.

In practice, digital marketing agencies often deliver monthly spreadsheets containing *impressions, views, CTR, engagement rates, and ROAS* without answering the only business question the client cares about:

> **"What actually caused our performance changes, and how should we adjust our media mix next month?"**

Without a standardized logic model, a client might see a month-over-month decline in engagement and falsely assume the creative team is failing. By separating paid and organic attribution, the actual scenario emerges:

| Metric Tracker | Surface-Level Client Interpretation | Actual Data-Backed Finding |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Organic Engagement Rate** | Declining quality | Stable baseline |
| **Paid Ad Spend (Media Buying)** | Ignored in context | Increased 240% on Broad Broad audiences |
| **Organic Reach (Algorithm)** | Weak distribution | Flat / Consistent |
| **Paid Reach (Amplification)** | Hidden driver | Dominant traffic source |
| **Audience Growth Diagnosis** | Content production problem | Media budget allocation problem |

The recommendation changes completely. Instead of *"Create better videos,"* the data-driven recommendation becomes *"Reduce low-efficiency Top-of-Funnel paid acquisition and reallocate budget toward high-performing Spark Ads."*

I built this system as **productized reasoning**. It is a reusable analytical framework that converts fragmented TikTok data into scalable client delivery.

